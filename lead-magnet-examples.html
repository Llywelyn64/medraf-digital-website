Five kinds of interactive tool

A good lead magnet gives a visitor something useful in a couple of minutes, whether or not they get in touch. Here are the five kinds, with working examples you can try.

A check

A short set of questions that shows someone where they stand. They answer in a couple of minutes and get an honest read on how they are doing, with one thing worth looking at next. It suits people who are not yet sure they need you. Seeing where they stand is what makes them realise they could do better, and you are the person who shows them how.

A calculator

The visitor enters three or four numbers they already know, and sees one they did not: what something is costing them, what a change would save, how long until a point is reached. The figure does the persuading. When someone sees the number for themselves, they can see the problem is real, and booking a call to deal with it is the obvious next step.

Try the Lost Enquiry Calculator →

A readiness checklist

A short list of what needs to be in place before a client is ready for your service. They tick off what they have, and see where the gaps are. When someone finds a gap, they can see they need help, and booking a call with you is the obvious next step. The ones who are ready arrive already prepared. The ones who are not know what to fix, and that you are the person to fix it.

A chooser

A few questions about the visitor’s situation, then the one option that fits them, with the reason. It answers the question you get asked most, which of your services is right for me, before they have to ask it. They stop guessing, you stop explaining the same options to everyone, and they arrive at the call already pointed at the right thing.

Try the one you just used →

A planner

A few questions about where the visitor is and what matters most, and they leave with a short, ordered plan they can follow. It suits people who know what they want but not where to start. The plan is useful on its own, and that is the point: it shows them you understand their situation, so doing it properly with your help is the natural next step. A simple version is filled in once and kept. One that saves progress between visits is a larger build, priced on its own.
