Where the research comes from

In a 2011 Harvard Business Review study, Oldroyd, McElheran and Elkington tested how quickly 2,241 US companies responded to a web-generated enquiry. Twenty-three per cent never responded at all. Among companies that did respond within 30 days, the average response time was 42 hours.

Earlier research by James Oldroyd and InsideSales, conducted while Oldroyd was at MIT Sloan, found that the odds of qualifying a web-generated lead were 21 times higher when the first call was attempted within five minutes rather than after 30 minutes.

A much larger InsideSales study published in 2021 analysed 5.7 million inbound leads and more than 55 million sales activities across more than 400 companies. It found conversion rates were more than eight times higher when the first attempt was made within five minutes compared with waiting between five minutes and 24 hours.

A 2022 Chili Piper study of B2B website demo enquiries also found that slow or missing responses remained common. Eighty per cent of companies took longer than five minutes to respond or did not respond at all, and nearly 30 per cent never responded.

These studies looked at particular types of web-generated sales enquiries and follow-up. They do not tell us what any individual business is losing. The calculator above uses your own figures and assumptions to illustrate what may be at stake.
